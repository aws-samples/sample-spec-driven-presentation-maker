Who this is for
A slide here is read in one second, from the back of a room, by someone who gets no handout. One beat lands per slide, so the beat is the whole slide: a word, a number, or a two-line claim.

Every graphic device is gone. At this scale a shape, a rule, or a box competes with the only thing carrying the message, so position replaces the box, grey replaces the bullet, and reading order replaces the arrow.
What that rules out
Red is only ever a word or a number — never a fill, a bar, an underline, or a rule. This style owns no shape and no line at all.

A poster look that needs a red block or a grid of rules is a different style; do not grow one into the other. What this one has instead is scale: 384px of black on white, and one word that is not black.
THE LIMITS
Character budget per line, weight 900

Over budget, a line does not shrink: it breaks into two lines, or it bleeds off the right edge on purpose.
Counts per slide and per deck
Deck: 8–18 slides. Stacks: 3 words, or 3–5 agenda items.
Red: 1 item per slide — a word or a number, never both.
Support copy: 3 lines per block; 1 block per slide, or 1 per column plus the synthesis at D.
Support widths: 800px at anchors A and C, 1760px at D, 1500px at B.
More than 3 lines only on the reference slides of Parts 2–6, at 12, and the text-heavy pattern, at 6.
Shapes 0, rules 0, boxes 0, images 0, rotation 0.
DO / DON'T
Do
Make one claim per slide and make the largest type that claim.
Let display type cross the left edge; -24px is the standard bleed.
Rank with position, size, and grey — first, largest, and black is the point.
Move the support block between the four anchors so no two consecutive slides look alike.
Keep body copy at #111111 and 18pt or larger.
Keep the left margin at 80px and the caption line at 920px.
Don't
No shape, rule, divider, box, panel, or icon — not even a hairline.
No red fill, bar, or underline; red is a word or a number.
No second accent, gradient, shadow, rotation, bullet character, or step number.
Never use #BBBBBB below 100pt or for body copy: at 1.9:1 on white it may only carry display words restated in black nearby. Where every word must stay legible, use #666666 at 5.7:1 instead.
Never shrink display type to fit a sentence; cut the sentence.
ONE BEAT
Title grammar
A title here is a noun or a two-word verb phrase, never a sentence: 12 capitals is the ceiling at --fs-slide-title, 7 at --fs-cover-title.

So the claim lives in two parts. The display line names the beat; the support block states the assertion in sentences. The display line alone is only a label, so the support block is optional only on a cover or a divider.
Density
One claim, one display beat, one support block, one red item. Nothing else is on the slide.

A slide that needs two claims is two slides. A slide that needs six lines of reasoning is the text-heavy pattern, which drops its display line to --fs-slide-title to earn the room.

Numbers get their own pattern: the metric is the beat and the caption is one line.
8–18 SLIDES
Sequence
Open with the bleed cover and close with the same composition and different words, so the deck is bracketed by its two loudest slides.

Between them, alternate: a 384px beat, then quieter evidence at --fs-stack or --fs-slide-title. Two 384px slides in a row read as shouting.

A divider is one red word alone on white; the agenda slide, repeated with the black item moved down, is the only tracker.
Favoured forms
Strongest here: a single metric, a three-word focus stack, a before / after pair, a one-word divider. Workable: columns as word stacks, a trend as numbers placed by magnitude, a table as aligned tabular text.

Avoid anything whose meaning is in a drawn form — a cycle, a tree, a swimlane, a dashboard. Faking one with type reads as a missing diagram, so use another style for those decks.

What the renderer cannot do
Slide JSON has no letter-spacing and no line-height: the tracking and the 0.9 line height here are demo-only. In a built deck, budget one line as em x 1.2 and expect display lines to sit further apart.
Only weights 400, 600, 800, and 900 exist; there is no intermediate weight to soften a display line with.
A native table inherits the template font, so the aligned-text table in Part 7 keeps this style and a native table does not.
