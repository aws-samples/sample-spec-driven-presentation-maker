Design decision
A point must land in one second at the back of a room, without a handout. One beat therefore fills each slide: a word, number, or two-line claim.

Scale carries the message. Position replaces boxes, grey replaces bullets, and reading order replaces arrows. Red is only a word or number—never a fill, bar, underline, or rule.

Every graphic device would compete with the beat. A poster needing blocks or ruled grids is another style.
Hard limits
Display budget: 7 capitals / 5 Japanese characters at 192pt; 10 / 7 at 120pt; 12 / 9 at 100pt; 22 / 16 at 56pt. Over budget, break the line or bleed right—never shrink.

Per slide: one claim, one red item, one support block; stacks hold 3 words or 3–5 agenda items. Support is ≤3 lines per block, except 6 on text-heavy slides and 12 on Parts 2–6 reference slides.

Shapes 0, rules 0, boxes 0, images 0, rotation 0.
DO / DON'T
Do
Make one claim per slide; make its beat the largest type.
Bleed display type from −24px; keep support inside at least 5% of the edge.
Rank with position, size, and grey: first, largest, and black is the point.
Move support among anchors A–D; do not repeat an anchor on consecutive slides.
Keep body copy #111111 at 18pt+ and contrast ≥4.5:1 (3:1 at 18pt+).
Keep display alignment at 80px and the caption line at 920px.
Charts, only if unavoidable, use native objects, direct labels, and no uninformative gridlines.
Don't
No shape, rule, divider, box, panel, icon, or emoji—not even a hairline.
No red fill, bar, underline, second accent, gradient, shadow, rotation, bullet, or step number.
Never use #BBBBBB below 100pt or for body copy: at 1.9:1 it may only carry display words restated nearby in black. Use #666666 at 5.7:1 when every word must remain legible.
Never shrink display type to fit a sentence; cut the sentence.
Never add a second claim, comparator number, or decorative chart; split the slide or choose another style.
ONE BEAT
Message grammar and density
The display title is a noun or two-word verb phrase, never a sentence. It names the beat; a support block states the assertion. Support is optional only on covers and dividers.

Use one display beat, one support block, one red word or number, and one claim. A second claim becomes a second slide. Six lines require one paragraph at --fs-slide-title, no bullets; a dim right block may hold only a supplied source. Metric: one beat, one caption line.
Chapter shape and favoured forms
Open and close with the two-line bleed composition. Alternate a 384px beat with quieter evidence at 120pt or 100pt; two 384px slides in sequence read as shouting.

A divider is one red word on white. For long reviews, repeat the agenda with the black item moved down; it is the only tracker. Deck length follows audience, time, and material.

Prefer a single metric, three-word focus stack, before/after pair, one-word divider, word columns, values positioned by magnitude, and aligned texts. Avoid cycles, trees, swimlanes, dashboards, and anything whose meaning depends on a drawn form.
