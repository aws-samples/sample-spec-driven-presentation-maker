Style guide · Teaching decision
Part 2 · 1/3
The page must teach after the instructor leaves
The learner revisits the deck alone, controls the pace, and needs to recover both context and reasoning without hearing the original narration.
DO
Keep module and step labels.Detached pages retain context.
Repeat define → example → practise.Repetition lowers switching cost.
Draw short source lines.Learners can trace evidence.
Keep body copy at 18pt+.Density must remain readable.
DO NOT
Avoid one-word theatrics.Pages must explain alone.
Keep steps and diagram labels.Context enables review.
Caption every image.Visuals must teach.
Use only two accents.Each already has a role.
Source / footnote area — keep the citation short; place full URLs in notes when needed.
Style guide · Teaching rules
Part 2 · 2/3
Each slide signals what kind of learning happens next
Use a definition, “How to …”, or “Can you …?” title.It signals the mental action.
Give definitions a distinct block.Canonical wording stays findable.
Label worked material “Example”.Separate rule from application.
Number every procedure.Order is instructional content.
Number exercises and show answer space.Invite work, not passive reading.
End with 3–5 takeaways.Bounded recall supports review.
Make the title the one claim.Two conclusions dilute both.
Use only warm emphasis and muted structure.Colour encodes function.
The title is the claim; the source line is content; page numbers and footers are template-owned.
Rule of thumb — if the slide cannot be understood in isolation, add context before adding decoration.

Style guide · Title grammar
Part 3 · 2/3
Titles name the learner’s next cognitive move
Use a complete definition, an actionable “How to …” phrase, or a “Can you …?” check. Keep it to two lines and one claim.
Good
A boundary is where responsibility changes
How to test an assumption in four steps
Can you explain the result without the diagram?
Bad, and why
Boundaries topic only; no claim
Next one-word theatre; no context
Several useful things to remember vague and plural
The title is the claim. If the evidence supports a different conclusion, rewrite the title rather than adding a second headline.
Grammar test — read only the titles; they should reconstruct the lesson objective, method, and check.
Style guide · Visual selection
Part 3 · 3/3

Purpose
Favour
Avoid
Numbers — large value and direct label. Avoid unlabeled metrics or decorative grids.
Comparison — aligned columns with shared criteria. Avoid cards whose rows cannot be compared.

Module 2 · Lesson 3 · Define
Lesson 3 · 1/6
A boundary is where responsibility changes
Definition
A boundary is a point where ownership, rules, or expected behaviour pass from one part of a system to another.
What belongs in the concept
A named change in responsibility plus the rule that governs the handoff.
What does not belong
A decorative dividing line with no change in ownership or behaviour.
