The gradient points at one thing
Find the thing the title is talking about; mark only that.
Design decision
A room that has to agree needs to know, in one glance, which element the agreement is about. The gradient is spent on that element and nothing else; the flatness of everything else is what makes it readable.
The ground and the 40 px edge bar arrive with the style: draw neither, and never set a slide background. In the preview PNG the figure and the outlines look solid violet — that is correct; add nothing to compensate.
The four pointer roles, and only four
The key number. Gradient text on that textbox, horizontal, 2 stops.
The chosen item. A 4 px outline plus one filled pill; siblings flat and muted.
The highlighted bar. Gradient fill on that bar alone, vertical, 2 stops.
No claim. A cover, agenda or divider takes the signature bar and nothing else.
One subject may be marked twice when a number and its bar are the same fact.
Rules that follow from the pointer
Ask what the slide claims, then which element carries it.
Do
  • Name the target in the title, then mark it.
    A title naming nothing gets the edge bar only.
  • Keep the comparison flat but for the winner.
    A recommendation shows only if the rest are quiet.
  • Let the count follow the content.
    Two claims means two slides; one fact may take two marks.
  • Keep edges crisp: no blur, no glow, no fade.
    A soft edge reads as an effect, not as a label.
Do not
  • No gradient panel, tile or full-bleed field.
    A large area points at everything, so at nothing.
  • No gradient behind or under body text.
    Small gradient type loses colour and contrast.
  • No second colour family, no emoji.
    One gradient plus grey is the whole system.
  • No gradient as decoration or a repeated tag.
    Once it decorates it stops meaning "look here".
The title names what the gradient marks

Title grammar. Write the claim as a sentence that contains the target: "Run cost falls by 42%" points at a figure, "Shared ownership is the recommended model" points at a card. A topic label such as "Costs" points at nothing and leaves the slide without a pointer.
Deck shape. Open on the strongest claim, not on an agenda. A section divider and the closing slide carry no pointer. Keep one claim per slide: if two elements both deserve the gradient, the slide is two slides.

The case for a bounded first phase
The evidence supports starting; it does not yet support expanding.
The pilot cleared the cost threshold and removed the hand-offs that produced lost requests. Its scope — one team, twelve weeks — is exactly what the first phase repeats, so the plan carries no new unknowns and the measures it will be judged on already exist.
Adoption outside the pilot team has not been shown. Expansion funding should therefore depend on a measured gate, a named owner and a review date rather than on a single approval taken today, when the evidence for the wider case does not yet exist.
Approve phase one; make expansion conditional on the gate.
