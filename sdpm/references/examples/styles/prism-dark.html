The gradient points at one thing
Find the element the title names; mark only that.
Design decision
A room reaching agreement must see which element the decision is about in one glance. Spend the gradient on that target; flat supporting content makes the pointer legible.
Do
  • Name the target in an assertion title.
  • Keep siblings flat and equal.
  • Keep text and pills horizontal, bars vertical, and every edge crisp: no glow, blur or fade.
  • Keep text contrast at 4.5:1; 3:1 at 18pt or larger.
Do not
  • No gradient panels or full-bleed fields: they point everywhere.
  • No gradient behind body text: contrast suffers.
  • No second colour family, repeated gradient tags, or gradient quota to spend.
  • No emoji; use one meaningful asset.
  • No second claim; split the slide.
One subject may be marked twice only when figure and chart mark express the same fact. Preview may flatten gradient text and outlines to violet; do not compensate.

Title grammar. Use a sentence with a concrete object: “Run cost falls by 42%” names a metric; “Shared ownership is the recommended model” names a choice. Never use a topic label such as “Costs.”
Chapter shape. Open on the strongest claim. A long-review agenda uses a noun-phrase title, hairline rows and a flat current pill—no pointer or highlighted entry. Use flat dividers and one closing ask. Prefer key figures, equal comparisons, processes, native charts and spare tables.
