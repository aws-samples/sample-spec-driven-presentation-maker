Rules / read first
Comparability is the design decision, not decoration
Leaders revisit these pages on screen and paper, comparing periods faster than they read prose. Stable geometry and restrained ink therefore outrank novelty.
Do
  • State one claim as a two-line maximum title.The result must arrive before the exhibit.
  • Keep edge margins at least 5% and geometry stable.Print, annotation, and cross-page scanning need clear space.
  • Label values directly; keep only informative baselines.Readers compare marks, not legends or empty gridlines.
  • Keep contrast ≥ 4.5:1, or ≥ 3:1 at 18pt+.Core evidence must remain readable.
Do not
  • Use rounded cards, shadows, 3D, or decorative colour.The white field and rules organize the page.
  • Use accent to mean good or bad.Accent means current period or selected series; signs state direction.
  • Place a large number without a comparator.Pair it with prior, plan, benchmark, or trend.
  • Use emoji or icons inside data exhibits.Glyph failure and decoration interrupt numeric scanning.
Limits: native tables inherit the template font. Slide JSON has no letter-spacing, line-height, or intermediate font weights; use explicit box heights and regular or bold weight.

Chapter shape
  • Cover → 3–5 executive findings → 4–6 KPI scorecard.
  • Use 2–3 evidence chapters: shared result, drivers, then segments.
  • Close with owned actions and next-period measures; move detail to an appendix whose glossary is alphabetical, with plain definitions and supplied source/scope notes.
  • Let audience, time, and material determine deck length.
Page density and visual choice
  • One claim and 25–55 visible words on a typical analytical page.
  • Prefer KPI rows, exact-value tables, native charts, shared-scale small multiples, rankings, bridges, and numbered findings; use stages only when sequence explains a metric, and sparse lines or a matrix only for explicit causality.
  • Open chapters with a question; close with a takeaway or action, not a recap.
  • Split independent clauses rather than shrinking type or adding panels.
