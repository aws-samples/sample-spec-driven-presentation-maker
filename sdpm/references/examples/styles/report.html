Illustrative style guide
Rules / read first
Comparability is the design decision, not decoration
Leaders read this report on screen and on paper, return to it each period, and compare pages faster than they read prose. Stable geometry and restrained ink therefore outrank novelty.
Always
  • Write one claim as the slide title.Reason: readers get the result before the exhibit.
  • Keep every edge margin at least 5%.Reason: print and annotations need clear space.
  • Use colour only to distinguish data.Reason: meaning must survive greyscale.
  • Keep contrast ≥ 4.5:1; ≥ 3:1 at 18pt+.Reason: review material must remain readable.
Across every exhibit
  • Label values directly; remove empty gridlines.Reason: eyes compare marks, not legends.
  • Use one baseline; add target or zero only if needed.Reason: axes orient; they do not decorate.
  • Accent current period or selected series.Outline emphasis selects the series; history stays grey.
  • Use no emoji; keep sourced icons outside tables.Reason: glyphs fail and icons disrupt numeric scanning.
Renderer facts: native tables inherit the template font and accept no per-table family. JSON has no letter-spacing, line-height, or 650 weight: approximate with bold and explicit box heights.
Rules / derived practice
Data ink earns space only when it improves comparison
Do
  • Repeat the finding, metric/unit, and period header.Reason: readers compare exhibits without relearning frames.
  • Make tables primary: align figures, rule rows, bold totals, and sign changes.Native tables use the template font; alignment carries the style.
  • Open with 4–6 contextualised KPIs.With no history, omit delta/sparkline and state “first period”.
Do not
  • Use 3D, decorative charts, rounded cards, or shadows.Leave shadows off.
  • Use decorative colour or a second accent.Accent means current period or selected series.
  • Place a large number alone.Always pair it with prior, plan, benchmark, or trend.
Title test: use a quantified finding, not a topic label.
Message and outline / title grammar
A finding title makes the page useful before the chart is read
Use a declarative sentence: subject + directional verb + quantified or bounded implication. Keep it to two lines and 8–14 words where possible.
Good: states the finding
Revenue grew 12% on a flat cost base
Three regions closed half of the plan gap
Cycle time improved, but the target remains unmet
Bad: names the exhibit
Revenue overview
Regional performance
Cycle time chart
One claim per slide. Put 25–55 visible words on a typical analytical page, excluding labels and tables. If two independent “and” clauses survive, split the page. Numbers substantiate the title; they do not replace it.
Grammar remains plain, specific, and comparable across reporting periods.

Visual choice
Numbers: KPI row, table, waterfall, direct-labelled bars.
Comparison: small multiples, variance table, ranking.
Process: simple left-to-right stages only when sequence explains a metric.
Relationship: sparse lines or matrix only when causality is explicit.
Text: numbered findings or short evidence paragraphs.
Avoid decorative timelines, dense networks, and unauditable diagrams.
Palette / one semantic accent
Colour means current period; grey carries everything else
Accent identifies the current period, selected series, or final total. An outline-emphasised figure takes precedence as the selected series; neutral structure stays distinct in greyscale.

Usage limits
  • One accent on one slide, never a decorative second colour.A fixed meaning preserves cross-page comparison.
  • Do not encode positive and negative by red and green.Use explicit +/− signs and words so print and accessibility do not erase meaning.
  • Use solid fills only.Flat greys and rules reproduce consistently in the renderer and on paper.

Appendix / definitions
Definitions keep period comparisons consistent
Metric glossary and scope
Reporting period
Use exact formulas, inclusions, exclusions, and reporting cut-off
Current period
The reporting window ending on the stated cut-off date; accent colour always maps to this period.
Completed units
Count of units meeting the completion rule within the period; excludes cancelled and duplicate records.
Gross margin
Revenue less direct cost, divided by revenue. State whether values are reported or adjusted.
Prior period
The immediately preceding comparable window using the same cut-off and scope rules.
Quality score
Composite measure using the documented weighting; include formula and target owner in the source notes.
Retention
Eligible entities remaining at period end divided by eligible entities at period start.
Source: metric dictionary. Definition: illustrative wording; replace with governed definitions.
