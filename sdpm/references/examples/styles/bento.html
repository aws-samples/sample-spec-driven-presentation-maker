Rules / read first
One container, one fact, one grid
This deck is scanned before it is read, often on a screen shared with people who joined late. Each fact therefore gets its own rounded container on a quiet grey ground, so any tile can be entered first and still make sense. Order is a reading convenience, never a prerequisite.
Every slide, every style
  • State one claim per slide; the title is that claim.
  • Keep every edge margin at 5% of the slide or more.
  • Keep text contrast at 4.5:1 or better; 3:1 only at 18pt and above.
  • Label chart values directly; drop gridlines that carry no information.
  • Use no emoji; use sourced icons only when a word cannot do the work.
Fixed numbers
  • Tiles per content slide: 3 minimum, 6 maximum.
  • Gap between tiles: 24px, horizontally and vertically, always.
  • Corner radius: 20px on every tile, whatever its size.
  • Dark tiles: exactly 1 per slide. Accent tiles: 0 or 1, never 2.
  • Text in one tile: one fact, 12 words or fewer.
  • Rotation: 0 degrees. Tiles are never tilted or overlapped.
Rules / derived practice
Restraint is what keeps it readable
Do
  • Place 3 to 6 tiles and let their sizes differ.Size is the hierarchy: the largest tile carries the claim's evidence.
  • Snap every tile to the 12-column grid and the 220px row.Column 118px, gap 24px; a span of n columns is 142n - 24 px.
  • Give each tile a label, a value and one qualifying line.Keep the fact itself to 12 words or fewer.
Don't
  • Do not nest a tile inside a tile, or overlap two tiles.A split fact takes a second tile on the grid, not an inner box.
  • Do not vary the gap, the radius or the shadow for emphasis.Emphasis is size, the dark tile and the accent tile.
  • Do not draw arrows or connectors between tiles.Order is left to right, top to bottom; a numeral carries sequence.
Test every tile
Can this tile be read alone, in one breath, and still say something true? If not, merge it with its neighbour or give it a slide.

Palette / five roles
Grey ground, white tiles, two voices
Ground / #F2F2F5
The deck ground behind every tile. Never used as a tile fill.
Tile / #FFFFFF
The default tile. Text on it is #1D1D1F at 15:1.
Dark tile / #1D1D1F
Exactly one per slide. White text; secondary lines #A1A1A6.
Accent tile / #3B5BFF
At most one per slide. White text only, no muted grade.
Muted text / #66666B
Labels, captions and chart bars at #C9C9D1. Text only, never a tile fill.
The dark tile marks the anchor of the slide: the claim, the synopsis or the conclusion. The accent tile marks the one thing that changed, was chosen or must be remembered.
Two accents cancel each other, so the second becomes a white tile. Colour never carries meaning alone; the label in the tile says what it is.

The band above the grid
Section label at 62px. Title box at 118px, height 160px: two lines at 30pt. A one-line title leaves the second empty and the grid never moves up.
Margins
Side margins 120px, top margin 62px, bottom margin 72px: every edge is above 5% of the slide.
The grid
12 columns of 118px, 3 rows of 220px, gap 24px. Column n starts at 120 + 142(n - 1); a span of n columns is 142n - 24 px.
Tile construction
Radius 20px: adjustment = 20 divided by the tile's shorter side.
A 220px-tall tile takes 0.09; a 402 x 464 tile takes 0.05.
Shadow: blur 8, distance 2, direction 90, colour #000000, opacity 0.08.
Padding 40px; 24px at 220px tall, 48px at 464px and above.
Only the label and the title sit on the ground; a source is the last line of its own tile.
