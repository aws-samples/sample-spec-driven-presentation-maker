Rules / do
Contrast carries hierarchy
  • Use one hard edge and one of four splits.
  • Put one claim on one field, proof on the other.
  • Use one sentence-case assertion title: six words, two lines; three short lines only in a narrow field.
  • Inset each field 96px—over 5% of the slide.
  • Orange words belong only on dark; all orange-field type is near-black.
  • Keep contrast ≥4.5:1, or ≥3:1 for 18pt+ text and non-text marks.
  • Label chart values directly; keep only informative baselines.
Do not / numeric limits
Back-row reading is the constraint: contrast replaces grids, cards, shadows and decorative colour.
  • No second edge, crossing, card, panel, badge or third colour.
  • No white or grey on orange; no grey body copy.
  • No shadow, radius, rotation, decorative icon or emoji.
  • No second claim, legend when labels fit, or empty gridline.
  • 1 split of 4; 2 fields + white; 2 body blocks/field plus meta.
  • 30–70 words; 3–5 native chart marks; 96px inset.
  • Grey is dark-field meta only at 11–16pt.
Ceilings, not targets: needing another block means another slide. Contrast: white/dark 16.8:1; near-black/orange and orange/dark 5.2:1; grey/dark 7.4:1. Use the minimum colours that distinguish content.
Message / title grammar
The edge is the argument
Decide which field holds the claim before writing; the opposite field proves it. The title is a sentence-case assertion, not a topic label. A second claim needs a second slide.
A 1088px field fits about 22 Latin or 13 Japanese characters per line: use two lines. A 448px field fits 9 and 5: allow three short lines. Leave unused title space empty.
One claim / 30–70 words
Count the title. Two fields hold claim and proof. The brief and material set deck length.
Choose form after claim
Use 67% for long evidence, 50:50 for one measure or comparison, horizontal for a wide table or sequence.
Chapter shape
Open on the strongest claim. Order claims before assigning splits. Use a title slide—not a divider graphic—at chapter changes. Turn the edge every three or four slides. Prefer a key figure, matched comparison, short process, relationship, native chart or spare table. Close on one ask with owner and timing.
