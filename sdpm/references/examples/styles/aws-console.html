aws-console
Evidence is organised like a live AWS work area

Three information-bearing bands establish place; dense widgets expose metrics, resources, state, and the next action.

The chrome carries section, slide index, date, presenter, agenda, and open-action state.
Console chrome carries deck context
Teams review operations, architecture, cost, and delivery on screen. A faithful work area keeps location, state, and action visible; controls must therefore inform, never decorate.
Frame rules
  • Use the complete three-band frame on cover, content, section, and closing slides.
  • Search text names the current section; its shortcut position carries the slide index.
  • Use date or venue as region text; presenter or team as account label.
  • The favourites row is the agenda: 3–7 section chips, each a fixed-colour 32px square with its number then section name; current text is white, others muted. Never put AWS services here.
  • Put services only inside a container when discussed, as a row chip plus linked service name. Never list them for decoration or to signal AWS; the frame already does that.
  • Repurpose every visible control; omit dead chrome.
Content rules
  • State one claim per slide; the assertion title is that claim.
  • Place evidence in 1–4 equal-width, never-nested containers.
  • Use 2px grey borders, 16px radius, no shadow, rotation, gradient, or decorative colour.
  • Give each container a 72px header with drag, Info, kebab, and resize affordances.
  • Prefer 56px rows, short labels, tables, metrics, fields, and chips to prose.
  • Use at most one orange primary action and one alert; blue means link, metric, or selection.
  • Keep contrast ≥ 4.5:1, or ≥ 3:1 at 18pt+; keep edge margins ≥ 5%.
  • No emoji. Use the fewest colours that distinguish content.

Native tables inherit the template font. Slide JSON has no letter-spacing, line-height, or intermediate font weights; reserve explicit heights and use regular or bold. Native charts label values directly, share meaningful baselines, and omit non-informative gridlines.

The title resolves one operational question
Title grammar
  • Write a short assertion sentence in sentence case.
  • Keep it to one line at 32pt: ≤18 CJK or ≤55 Latin characters.
  • With two actions, keep the claim within 1180px; shorten rather than wrap.
  • The title is the claim, not a topic label.
  • Good: The selected path meets the target
Shape and density
  • Let audience, time, and material set deck length.
  • Use 1–4 containers per slide.
  • Use one table, list, metric, or chip grid per body.
  • Keep prose to at most two lines.
  • Content always begins at y 300; never move it up under a short title.
  • Lead with the evidence state; close with owner, measure, timing, and one action.
Outline and forms
  • No fixed chapter sequence: the favourites agenda follows the material.
  • Open on the decision; use console destinations as section dividers.
  • Prefer dashboards, cost widgets, resource tables, service lists, comparisons, create wizards, grouped architecture, flashbars, side navigation, and pagination.
  • End with accountable execution, not a recap.
