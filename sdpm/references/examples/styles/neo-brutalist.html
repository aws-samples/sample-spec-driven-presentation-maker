Style / any deck
Rules / read first
Blunt hierarchy is the decision
The audience must grasp the claim at a glance in any setting. Heavy structure, hard contrast, and a few flat fills make hierarchy undeniable; restraint keeps force from becoming noise.
Always
  • One claim per slide; the title is the claim.
  • Keep every edge margin at least 5%.
  • Keep text contrast at least 4.5:1; 3:1 only for 18pt or larger.
  • Label chart values directly; remove non-informative gridlines.
  • Use no emoji; use sourced icons only when essential.
Fixed geometry
  • Border: 6px black, fixed.
  • Shadow: 12px hard offset, fixed; blur 0.
  • Corner radius: 0px, always.
  • Rotation: 0 degrees; no rotated elements.
  • Primary fills: maximum 3 colors per slide.
  • Labels: uppercase, maximum 3 words.
Rules / derived practice
Force comes from consistency
Do
  • Build every filled box with a 6px black border.The border makes each block legible before color does.
  • Apply one native hard shadow to every filled box.Use {type: outer, blur: 0, distance: 12, direction: 45, color: #111111, opacity: 1}.
  • Choose up to 3 primary fills for the slide.Repeat colors by role; do not spend all four at once.
Don't
  • Do not soften corners, borders, or shadows.Radius stays 0px; border stays 6px; shadow stays 12px.
  • Do not rotate, skew, overlap, or add gradients.The permitted rotation is exactly 0 degrees.
  • Do not write long labels or decorative tags.Labels stay uppercase and no longer than 3 words.
Test every choice: does it sharpen the claim, or merely make the slide louder?
Message and outline
Short claims hit harder
Title grammar
Use a punchy assertion of 6 words or fewer. Keep it to 2 lines maximum. Draft “Claim of this slide stated as a sentence,” then compress without losing the claim.
Deck shape
Let the brief and content set the length. Use one claim per slide. Opening, sequence, and closing follow the material; this look imposes no chapter template.
Density limit
Use 15–45 visible words on a typical slide, excluding labels and tables. Show 1–4 content blocks. Split the slide when a second independent claim appears.
Favor large numbers, direct comparisons, short processes, explicit relationships, and compact tables. The subject may vary; the visual grammar does not.
