Rules / read first
Blunt hierarchy is the decision
The claim must land at a glance in any setting. Heavy structure, hard contrast, and few flat fills make hierarchy undeniable; restraint keeps force from becoming noise.
Do
  • One claim per slide; the title is the claim.
  • Keep edge margins ≥ 5%; contrast ≥ 4.5:1, or ≥ 3:1 at 18pt+.
  • Label chart values directly; remove non-informative gridlines.
  • No emoji; use sourced icons only when essential.
  • Give every filled box a 6px black border.The border works before color does.
  • Give it one native hard shadow.{type: outer, blur: 0, distance: 12, direction: 45, color: #111111, opacity: 1}.
Do not
  • Never soften corners, borders, or shadows.Radius 0px; border 6px; shadow 12px, blur 0.
  • Never rotate, skew, overlap, or add gradients.Permitted rotation is exactly 0°.
  • No long labels or decorative tags.Uppercase, 3 words maximum.
  • Choose ≤ 3 primary fills; repeat colors only by role.
Color rule: never use color as the only signal. Test every choice: does it sharpen the claim, or merely make the slide louder?
Message and outline
Short claims hit harder
Title grammar
Use a punchy assertion of 6 words or fewer, maximum 2 lines. Draft “Claim of this slide stated as a sentence,” then compress without losing the claim.
Deck shape
Brief and content set length. Keep one claim per slide. Opening, sequence, and closing follow the material; this look imposes no chapter template.
Density limit
Use 15–45 visible words, excluding labels and tables, in 1–4 content blocks. Split when a second independent claim appears.
Favor large numbers, direct comparisons, short processes, explicit relationships, and compact tables. Subject varies; visual grammar does not.
