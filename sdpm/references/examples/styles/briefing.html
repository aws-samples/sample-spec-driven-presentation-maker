Bundled style / technical briefing
Briefing
A short argument for people who decide in the room and verify the reasoning later.
Dark ground / one teal accent / noun-phrase title / stated claim / no cards
01 / Rules / design decision
Decision-room legibility
The speaker explains the evidence; the slide preserves one position for later review.
DO / because the argument must scan
  • Keep one claim per slide. The title-topic pair advances one position rather than listing several topics.
  • Use at least 5% margins. Room projection needs a stable reading edge and breathing space.
DO NOT / because decoration changes order
  • No cards. Repeated boxes fragment a spoken argument into tiles the eye reads out of order.
  • No gradients or shadows. Dark grounds muddy both; set shadows off.
Text contrast is at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 at 18pt and above. Structure comes from filled header bands, rules, washed panels, and whitespace—not enclosure.
Source: briefing design decision
01 / Rules / discipline
Evidence-first discipline
Direct labels and restrained colour keep attention on the evidence.
DO / because evidence must decode directly
  • Label values directly. Remove chart gridlines that carry no information.
  • Keep colour minimal. Add only the hue needed to distinguish content.
DO NOT / because unsupported marks distract
  • No emoji. Use monochrome icons from search_assets in the text colour.
  • No decorative state colour. Success and danger require a stated outcome or risk.
Neutrals structure the page; one meaningful mark directs attention.
Source: briefing discipline
01 / Rules / wording
Formal title grammar
A noun-phrase title names the issue; the topic sentence directly below states the one claim.
Use / formal noun phrases
  • Measured reduction in review time
  • Preferred operating model
  • Controlled rollout conditions
Avoid / questions and spoken phrasing
  • Can we move faster?
  • Let's look at the options
  • What this means for us
Questions, invitations, and conversational emphasis belong in speaker notes. Printed language remains direct, formal, and unambiguous after the meeting.
Closing insight stays plain text: the last line is spoken, not labelled.
Source: wording discipline

05 / Frame / standard
Fixed briefing frame
The title zone never moves; evidence geometry changes only below the rule.
Content region / x 120–1800 / y 330–960
Exact production geometry
Section label: x=120, y=28. Accent bar: 3×120 px. Title: x=120, y=58, width=1680, height=192 px for two 40pt lines. Topic sentence: y=258, height=58. Rule: y=322. Content begins at y=330.
Footer and template contract
One-line titles leave the second line empty; content never moves up. A source line is content and may be drawn at bottom left. The page number is the template's and is never drawn. Leave x=1400–1920, y=985–1080 clear.
No cards: the outline here marks the available region only. Production slides use bands, rules, washes, or whitespace as their reasoning requires.

Pattern / read-alone paragraphs
Sequence of evidence
Evidence moves from condition to turning point to bounded implication.
Observed condition. The current process distributes responsibility across several handoffs, so no single role sees the complete decision path or owns the recurring delay.
Measured turning point. A bounded trial removes two transitions while preserving the same threshold, scope, and review period; the improvement can therefore be attributed to the control change.
