Bundled style
Briefing
A dark style that speaks through spacing and rules. Structure the information; do not decorate it.
Dark ground · one teal accent · noun-phrase titles with a topic sentence · no cards
01
Rules · read first
Core rules of this style
The speaker explains; the slide states the claim and shows the evidence.
Do — because the room scans before it reads
Put the claim under the title.A re-reader gets the point without the speaker.
Separate with rules and space.Structure comes from alignment, not from boxes.
Numbers large, units small.The value is read from the back row; the unit up close.
Do not — because extra signals dilute the order
No cards; bands, rules, space.Cards fragment one argument into tiles read out of order.
No 2nd accent, shadows, emoji.Dark grounds muddy depth effects; one accent means one focus.
No topic labels or questions.A decision room is given positions, not prompts.
Do not enclose — separate. Do not pack — leave space. Do not decorate — align.
02
Message discipline
One-direction message depth
Title, topic sentence, facts, one implication — read top-left to bottom-right.
Rule 1 — state the claim
A title alone names a topic. The topic sentence says what to conclude.
Never ship a label-only slide. If there is no claim, there is no slide.
Rule 2 — order by depth
Facts first, interpretation below. Depth grows down the page, never sideways.
An implication without visible evidence above it is a claim the room cannot check.
Rule 3 — compress
Lead with the conclusion, cut dispensable words, never repeat a point.
A sentence that wraps because it carries two claims is a signal to split the slide.
Read only the topic sentences in order; they must form one continuous argument.
03
Wording discipline
Title grammar and register
Titles are formal noun phrases. The claim lives in the topic sentence, not in the title.
Not used as titles
"Should we migrate this quarter?"
"Let's talk about cost"
"Some thoughts on the roadmap"
Adopted
Migration timing
Operating cost structure after migration
Roadmap priorities for the next two quarters
Spoken register (default)
Short lines; the voice carries detail. ≤ 40 words per slide.
Written register (read alone)
Fuller sentences and notes; ≤ 90 words. Same frame, same patterns.
What is said out loud is not printed; what is printed must stand without the speaker.

Chapter shape. Open with the strongest claim, not an agenda. A divider with an oversized number marks each section. Close on one plain-text implication.
Visual forms. Numbers: problem → outcome. Comparison: two halves. Process: chevrons. Relationship: cycle or stages. Text: paragraph bars.

Frame
Title band, topic sentence, content, source
The topic sentence sits here, at y = 262, one line. Content starts at y = 350 on every slide.
Content region · x 120–1800 · y 350–950
Topbar: 0–240 px; the title box holds two lines at 40pt (192 px). One-line titles leave the second line empty — content never moves up.
Divide this region with hairlines and filled header bands. Columns: 2 × 800 (rule at x 960), 3 × 520 (gap 60), 4 × 390 (gap 40).
The source line is content: draw it at y = 985 when the slide cites something. The page number is the template's — leave the bottom-right corner empty.

Pattern · process
Department onboarding in four steps
Every department follows the same four weeks; the plan repeats and the risk is known.
1 · Configure
2 · Migrate
3 · Train
4 · Switch over
Week 1 — approval chains and fields set up with the department lead.
Week 2 — open records moved; spreadsheets kept read-only.
Week 3 — two sessions per team; the department runs both systems.
Week 4 — new requests only in the tool; old ones close out.
Expected effect

Fund the first two departments now; release the rest when week-4 measures hold.

13
Pattern · section heads and bullets
Known risks and their mitigations
Both risks are bounded by a decision already taken, not by hope.
Historical records
  • Spreadsheets stay read-only for twelve months.
  • Only open requests are migrated.
  • Lookups fall back to the old file by link.
Custom approval chains
  • Two departments keep their chains, configured in week 1.
  • Chains are reviewed at the switch-over gate.
  • No exceptions after go-live.
Neither risk delays the first two departments. Both are reviewed once, at the gate, by the department lead.
14
Pattern · paragraphs
The case for a bounded start
The evidence supports a first phase now; expansion should wait for measured adoption.
The pilot cleared the economic threshold and removed the operational hand-offs that produced lost requests. Its scope — one department, twelve weeks — is exactly what the first phase repeats.
Adoption has not yet been shown outside the pilot team. Expansion funding should therefore depend on a measured gate, a named owner and a review date rather than on a single approval today.
Approve the first phase; make expansion conditional on adoption.
15
Pattern · pills and key band
What the pilot proved
Speed and reliability improved together; neither was traded for the other.
Speed
Median turnaround fell from 6.2 to 2.1 days. Requests over ten days fell from 31% to 4%.
Reliability
Lost requests fell from 9% to none. Satisfaction rose from 2.9 to 4.4 out of 5.
Key message
A three-times faster turnaround with zero lost requests, from one shared procedure.
