Bundled style
Briefing
A dark style that speaks through spacing and rules. Structure the information; do not decorate it.
Dark ground · one teal accent · noun-phrase titles with a topic sentence · no cards
Structure outranks decoration
A presenter speaks while the room scans; expose one claim and its evidence. Do not enclose—separate; do not pack—leave space; do not decorate—align.
Do — because the room scans first
  • Put one claim in the topic sentence below a formal noun-phrase title.A re-reader gets the point without the speaker.
  • Separate with rules, bands, alignment, and whitespace; keep margins at least 5%.Structure stays visible without fragmenting the argument.
  • Make numbers large and units small; label chart values directly and retain only informative baselines.Evidence reads from the back row without legends or empty gridlines.
  • Keep contrast at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 at 18pt and larger.Dark-ground evidence must remain legible.
Do not — extra signals dilute order
  • No cards: use open fields, lightly washed panels, bands, and rules.Boxes turn one argument into tiles read out of order.
  • No second accent, shadows, rounded decoration, 3D effects, or emoji.One accent means one focus; dark grounds muddy false depth.
  • No topic-label-only slide, question title, or conversational title.A decision room receives positions, not prompts.
  • Do not use green or coral unless success or danger is explicitly stated.Meaning colours are evidence, never decoration.
Title, topic sentence, facts, implication
Read top-left to bottom-right; depth grows down the page, never sideways.
1 · State the claim
A formal noun phrase names the subject. The topic sentence states what to conclude.
Never ship a label-only slide. If there is no claim, there is no slide.
2 · Order by depth
Facts first and interpretation below. Evidence must be visible before the implication.
Use problem → outcome for numbers and identical row order for comparisons.
3 · Compress
Lead with the conclusion, cut dispensable words, and never repeat a point.
If one sentence contains two claims or wraps excessively, split the slide.
Read only the topic sentences in order; they must form one continuous argument.
Wording, density, and chapter shape
Title grammar
Use formal business noun phrases: “Migration timing” or “Operating cost structure after migration.” Never print questions, invitations, or conversational language.
Topic sentence
Use subject + verb + implication in one line. Outline claims remain one sentence; the composer adds the noun-phrase title.
Density and register
  • One claim per slide; deck length follows audience, time, takeaway, and material.
  • Spoken briefing: short lines, at most 40 words; the voice carries detail.
  • Read-alone material: fuller topic sentences and supporting detail, at most 90 words.
  • Never shrink type to fit; edit, split, or change visual form.
Chapter and visual form
  • Open with the strongest claim, not an agenda.
  • Use oversized-number dividers between sections; close on one plain-text implication.
  • Prefer numbers with context left and the larger, wider outcome right; aligned comparisons; effect-labelled chevrons; ring cycles distinct from left-to-right stages; swimlanes; paragraph bars; native charts; and spare tables.
  • Written-only: use 2–3 filled-head sections with ≤4 unnested one-line bullets. Four alternatives use equal columns with common heads and metric footers; parts of one whole share one hairline-divided band.
  • For proportions use one gauge or ≤5 directly labelled same-scale bars; never mix gauge and donut.
  • Use a divider only for a true chapter pause; move supporting detail after the decision path.
Slide JSON cannot express letter-spacing, line-height, or intermediate font weights; use explicit box heights and regular or bold weight. Native tables inherit the template font.
