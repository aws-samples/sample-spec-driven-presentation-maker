The gradient points at one thing
Find the element the title names; mark only that.
Design decision
Mixed audiences in bright rooms must see the decision target in one glance. The white ground carries reading; one gradient marks the target, and flat support keeps it legible.
Four legal roles
  • Key figure: gradient text; its own chart mark may match.
  • Chosen option or step: 4px outline plus filled pill.
  • Named chart period: that native mark alone.
  • No claim: signature edge alone.
What stays flat
  • The 40px three-stop edge repeats on every slide.
  • Metadata may use an outline pill, never a filled emphasis.
  • Cards, connectors, labels and sibling marks use neutrals.
  • One subject may be marked twice only when a figure and its chart mark express the same fact.
  • There is no gradient quota to spend.
Preview may flatten gradient text and outlines to violet; do not compensate. Shape fills retain the ramp.
A gradient that decorates can no longer point
Do
  • Mark only what the assertion title names.A second subject makes the claim ambiguous.
  • Keep ramps to text, lines, pills and native chart marks.A bounded mark remains a pointer.
  • Keep direction fixed: horizontal for text and pills, vertical for bars.Direction is part of the visual grammar.
  • Keep siblings flat, equal and muted; use one family, regular/bold, no italics.One asymmetry identifies one target.
Do not
  • No full-bleed gradient, panel, tile or card fill.
  • No blur, glow, radial ramp or fade.
  • No hue outside the three stops.
  • No gradient for status or category.
  • No gradient behind body text; no emoji.
One claim per slide; margins at least 5%; contrast ≥ 4.5:1, or ≥ 3:1 at 18pt+ or 15pt bold; label values directly and remove gridlines that carry no information.
