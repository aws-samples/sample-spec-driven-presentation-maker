Broadsheet / reading copy
The deck is written
to be read
Fixed measures turn prose into a composed page, while one red mark carries emphasis.
The headline makes one claim. A short italic line tells the reader how the columns support it.
Each column is a separate text box. Read down, then across, without cards or decorative panels.
Paper is the surface. Ink, hairlines, and measured space hold the composition together.
Paper / ink / hairlines / one red
Rules / decision
Read alone
Print composition makes prose
the primary evidence
A circulated deck must carry its reasoning without a presenter or decorative cue.
Do
Let measure create order
Use sentence headlines, justified columns, direct labels, and straight rules. Keep margins beyond five per cent.
Do not
Add a second visual system
No cards, shadows, gradients, opacity, tilted rules, emoji, or fill behind prose.
If a page argues twice, split it before changing the type or grid.

Pattern / reading spread
Signature page
One drop cap opens prose
that must stand alone
Build the opening from three boxes: letter, three inset lines, then full measure below.
P
rose begins beside one cap for exactly three lines.
The first column then resumes at full measure. Its job is to establish the terms and evidence the next columns develop.
The second column advances the same argument at matched density. It gets no second cap and no visual detour.
The last column closes the reasoning and names the consequence. A second claim begins on another page.
